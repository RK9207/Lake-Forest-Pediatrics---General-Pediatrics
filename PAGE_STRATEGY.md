# Page strategy — General Pediatrics

## Objective and approved direction

Turn paid-search visits into appointment inquiries for Lake Forest Pediatrics. Build one General Pediatrics landing page for Lake Bluff, Lindenhurst, and Vernon Hills. Show visitors which office they are contacting and make calling the primary action. Use **Request Appointment**, which opens the GoHighLevel (GHL) appointment form in a popup, as the secondary action.

Lead with the office and services. Follow with a concise introduction to the people behind the care, answer practical questions, then bring the visitor back to the contact options. Use a compact, mobile-first layout for parents and caregivers searching for a nearby pediatrician or comparing practices for ongoing care.

## Section order and conversion journey

### 1. Hero — general pediatric care at the selected office

- **Purpose and visitor question** — Establish relevance immediately. “Is this a pediatric practice near me, and how can I reach it?”
- **Main message and content** — Show the practice name, general pediatric care, selected office, and a short value statement about finding care for ongoing needs. When the office is unknown, show the location selector.
- **Emotional and trust roles** — Help the visitor feel oriented. Ground the offer in a named practice and office.
- **Visual treatment** — Put the service, office, and actions together in a text-led hero. Use a real team or office photo only if it adds trust and fits the mobile reading sequence.
- **CTA and flow** — Carry the ad’s service and location into the primary **Call [phone number]** action. Put **Request Appointment** alongside it, then lead into a concise overview for visitors who need to check service fit.

### 2. Services and benefits

- **Purpose and visitor question** — Help families assess fit. “What can this practice help my child with?”
- **Main message and content** — Feature verified well-child visits, immunizations, newborn care, and school or sports physicals. Connect each to a practical benefit, such as discussing development, keeping up with preventive care, or preparing for school and sports. Routine sick-child care can be mentioned as part of ongoing care, with acute illness advertising kept in the separate campaign.
- **Emotional and trust roles** — Replace broad assurances with concrete examples parents can recognize and ask about.
- **Visual treatment** — Use a compact list with strong service labels and short explanations. Give the most relevant service the strongest emphasis once campaign inputs are available.
- **CTA and flow** — Expand on the hero promise, then move from what the practice provides to who provides it. Keep the hero and contact section as the strongest CTA moments.

### 3. Care-team proof

- **Purpose and visitor question** — Help families evaluate the practice. “Who might care for my child?”
- **Main message and content** — Include one brief provider or team feature using source-backed qualifications, such as board-certified pediatricians or more than 50 years of serving the area. Verify any individual role or office assignment.
- **Emotional and trust roles** — Help families picture their first interaction and give them an identifiable person, team, or practice to trust.
- **Visual treatment** — Pair an authentic photo with a short caption and proof point. Use a factual text-led feature if approved photo assets are unavailable.
- **CTA and flow** — Build on the services section and move from practice-provided credentials to feedback from families.

### 4. Google Reviews

- **Purpose and visitor question** — Provide social proof for families comparing practices. “What have other families experienced?”
- **Main message and content** — Show the confirmed Google rating and review count, approved relevant excerpts, and the verified profile link. Use the same verified rating in the hero badge.
- **Emotional and trust roles** — Reinforce confidence with specific feedback about communication or the visit experience. Preserve the reviews’ meaning and attribution.
- **Visual treatment** — Keep the section compact and show up to six reviews at once on desktop and three on mobile.
- **CTA and flow** — Offer **Read all our Google reviews**. Follow the proof with practical answers for families ready to take the next step.

### 5. Getting started

- **Purpose and visitor question** — Resolve likely barriers. “Can I make an appointment for my child, and what should I know first?”
- **Main message and content** — Answer the most relevant verified questions about new-patient availability, insurance, visit types, and scheduling. Explain any steps a family needs to take before contacting the practice.
- **Emotional and trust roles** — Reduce uncertainty about eligibility and effort. Give office-specific answers where policies vary.
- **Visual treatment** — Use a quieter text-led section with short answers and clear headings. Use an accessible accordion only where it improves scanning.
- **CTA and flow** — Carry the confidence built by the care-team section into the contact area. An answer may link to the form or office phone when it naturally calls for action.

### 6. Contact the selected office

- **Purpose and visitor question** — Convert interest into an inquiry. “Which office am I contacting, and what happens next?”
- **Main message and content** — Restate the selected office beside its call link and popup trigger. At the top of the popup, state the selected office and describe the verified next step. If inquiries are handled centrally, explain that accurately while keeping the requested office visible.
- **Emotional and trust roles** — Give families confidence that their office choice is recorded and set accurate expectations about follow-up.
- **Visual treatment** — Use a distinct contact section with the office and call action visible near the form. Show success or error feedback in place.
- **CTA and flow** — Repeat the hero’s primary and secondary actions with consistent labels. Give visitors a way to correct their location before submitting or calling. Lead into the full office directory.

### 7. Map and office directory footer

- **Purpose and visitor question** — Confirm local presence and help visitors find or switch offices. “Where are the offices, and how can I get to the right one?”
- **Main message and content** — Always list Lake Bluff, Lindenhurst, and Vernon Hills with the sourced addresses and directions. Include a responsive map with **Get Directions** near the directory, plus the brief’s privacy policy and practice website links in the footer.
- **Emotional and trust roles** — Reassure families that they can see all their office options and correct a mistaken selection.
- **Visual treatment** — Use a compact, consistent directory. Make the selected office identifiable in text as well as visually.
- **CTA and flow** — Offer directions or a way to change the selected office after the main contact area.
