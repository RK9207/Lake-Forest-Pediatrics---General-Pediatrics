# Lake Forest Pediatrics Sick Visits – Messaging

Nov 26, 2026 · @Rohit Kumar

## 1. Value proposition and voice

**Value proposition.** Lake Forest Pediatrics helps families find a local sick visit when their child has cold, flu, or RSV symptoms.

**Visitor outcome.**

- Clear hours and office-specific contact options help caregivers choose the next step quickly.
- On-site testing, when appropriate, may let families get an evaluation and any needed testing in one place.

**Reader.** An adult caregiver making a time-sensitive decision.

**Voice.**

- Use a warm, matter-of-fact voice.
- Acknowledge the situation once, then give practical answers.
- Favor plain language, short paragraphs, and headings that make sense when read in isolation.

## 2. Messaging hierarchy

| Priority | Message | Placement | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Service and fit | H1 and hero support | Lead with pediatric sick visits. Mention cold, flu, and RSV symptoms naturally. Keep the practice name and selected office visible near the call action. |
| 2 | Availability | Early section heading and three clearly labeled entries | Lead with same-day sick visits Monday through Saturday. Present Sunday drop-ins from 8–9 a.m. and the 24/7 care line as distinct options. |
| 3 | Visit benefit | One section heading and short explanation | Explain what an evaluation and on-site testing can mean for the family. Use an appropriate qualifier and link the capability to potentially getting evaluation and testing in one place. |
| 4 | Local trust | Image caption or brief proof element | Identify the practice, provider, team, or office shown. Use a verified review only if it answers a relevant concern and has approved usage. |
| 5 | Objections and logistics | FAQ and form microcopy | Answer practical questions about hours, after-hours access, testing, and where an inquiry goes. Add eligibility and insurance details only when verified. |

## 3. Hero and ad message match

### 3.1 Hero structure

| Element | Guidance |
| --- | --- |
| H1 | Use a pattern that names the service, such as **Pediatric sick visits for cold, flu, and RSV symptoms**. |
| Selected office | Put it in adjacent text, where it can be updated consistently with the CTA and form. |
| Supporting line | Explain availability or the next step, depending on the live ad promise. |
| Scheduling detail | Keep the exact scheduling distinctions in the availability section directly below. |

### 3.2 Message-match inputs

- **Approved primary theme** — Local pediatric sick visits for cold, flu, and RSV symptoms. It is an inferred search intent based on the campaign discussion.
- **Candidate language** — “Same-day pediatric sick visits,” “pediatric sick visits near me,” “cold, flu, and RSV symptoms,” and “on-site testing when appropriate.” Use the phrases where they answer a real question. Let the office label express locality rather than writing “near me” into page copy.
- **Campaign inputs** — The screenshots establish the campaign topic and care details. Check actual keywords and ad copy when available. Match the approved ad’s service, location, availability qualifiers, and call or inquiry action. Keep routine pediatric care on the separate General Pediatrics page. Keep symptom education and testing-only terms secondary.

## 4. Benefits and claims

| Supplied detail | Benefit to explain | Wording boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same-day sick visits Monday through Saturday | Families can ask about getting their child seen that day. | Describe availability without guaranteeing a slot. |
| Sunday drop-ins from 8–9 a.m. | Families can plan around a specific Sunday window. | Verify participating office or offices before attaching a location to the claim. |
| 24/7 care line | Caregivers have a way to reach the practice’s care line outside regular hours. | Confirm the number and instructions. Describe this as phone access and keep visit hours separate. |
| On-site testing | Evaluation and any needed testing may be available in one place. | Name individual tests, turnaround times, or other clinical details only if verified. |
| Three office-specific contact paths | Families can see which office they are contacting before they call or submit. | Keep the office, number, and form synchronized. Confirm whether submissions are handled locally or centrally before describing the routing. |

## 5. CTA, form, and FAQ guidance

### 5.1 CTA wording

| CTA | Label | Where and how |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | **Call for a sick visit** | Use in the hero and contact section. Keep the same wording near the availability summary if a repeated CTA is needed there. Show a real clickable number once supplied. |
| Secondary (in-page link) | **Send an inquiry** | Links to the form. |
| Secondary (form submit) | **Send inquiry** | Form submission button. |
| Final CTA | Call and inquiry together | Reaffirm the selected office and present the call and inquiry choices together. Give the call the stronger visual and verbal emphasis. |

Use “book” or “schedule online” only if the form actually confirms an appointment.

### 5.2 Before the form

- Name the selected office and explain the verified next step.
- Emphasize the phone option for time-sensitive scheduling.
- Use the practice’s approved fields and minimize the information collected.
- Keep medical details out of a general inquiry form unless the practice provides an approved intake process.

### 5.3 Form feedback

- Keep labels visible and write specific error messages.
- Acknowledge receipt after successful submission and explain what happens next using confirmed response expectations.
- Avoid implying the appointment is booked.

### 5.4 FAQ

- Start with same-day appointment availability, Sunday drop-in logistics, use of the 24/7 care line, and how the practice decides whether on-site testing is appropriate.
- Keep answers direct enough to scan on a phone.
- Use confirmed responses for new-patient eligibility and insurance if those questions are included.
