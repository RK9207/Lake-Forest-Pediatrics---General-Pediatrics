\#\# IMG-HERO-01 \- Hero

\#\#\# Placeholder purpose

Make a routine pediatric visit feel familiar. The image should help a caregiver picture a child taking part in an ordinary checkup. The heading, location selector, and call button already tell visitors what care is available and how to reach the practice.

\#\#\# Recommended source type

AI-generated lifestyle/editorial image appropriate

\#\#\# Recommended composition

\- \*\*Orientation\*\* — 4:3 landscape, ideally 1600 × 1200 px or larger.  
\- \*\*Subject placement\*\* — Keep the action and expressions close together in the upper middle of the frame. Angle the interaction inward or slightly left, toward the copy beside the image.  
\- \*\*Negative space and crop\*\* — Leave modest space around heads and meaningful gestures. Use quiet wall or room detail at the edges. The copy sits outside the photo, so reserve no text-overlay area. Keep essentials inside the middle 70% for the slot’s centered \`object-fit: cover\` crop.  
\- \*\*Desktop and mobile\*\* — The slot sits in the narrower right column on desktop and below the call, appointment, and office controls on mobile. The main cue should read even when only the top of the frame is visible above the sticky call button.

\#\#\# Option 1 \- A moment at the height chart

Create a photorealistic 4:3 landscape editorial photograph for a general pediatrics landing page. Show a child around 6–8 standing naturally at a wall-mounted stadiometer during a routine checkup. A pediatric clinician gently adjusts the headpiece while the child’s caregiver watches from beside them. Capture the child’s quiet curiosity and the caregiver’s interest, with everyone looking at the measurement or each other. Use a 40 mm, child-eye-level composition in a typical unbranded outpatient exam room, with soft window light, off-white walls, and restrained sage and pale-blue detail. Dress the family in everyday layers and the clinician in a simple scrub top. Keep the child’s face and the clinician’s gesture within the upper central area, with enough depth of field to read both. Render natural skin texture, hands, and scale. Keep markings indistinct and exclude visible text, logos, needles, and theatrical medical props.

\#\#\# Option 2 \- A newborn visit with room for questions

Create a photorealistic 4:3 landscape editorial photograph of an early newborn visit. A caregiver seated in a chair holds a clothed, sleeping newborn securely in their arms while asking a question. A pediatric clinician sits at the same level, listening with open posture and a relaxed expression. Use a side-on 50 mm documentary composition so the caregiver’s face, the baby’s supported body, and the clinician’s profile form a close group in the upper center. Use diffuse daylight, warm white and pale blue, a plain knit blanket, and ordinary unbranded clinical attire. Keep both adult expressions readable with moderate depth of field. Leave space above and beside the group for cropping. Exclude legible paperwork, screens, monitors, logos, and exaggerated smiles.

\#\#\# Option 3 \- A familiar trip through the neighborhood

Create a photorealistic 4:3 landscape lifestyle editorial photograph of a caregiver walking with a child around 9–11 on a quiet Midwestern suburban sidewalk on the way to a routine appointment. The child glances up mid-conversation with a small gesture, and the caregiver turns slightly to listen. Use a 35 mm side-on, waist-up or three-quarter frame with leafy street trees, soft late-morning light, and muted olive, denim blue, and cream. Place the two faces and gesture close together in the upper center with soft, unobtrusive background detail at the edges. Give the scene the texture and body language of an ordinary weekday. Avoid recognizable clinic buildings or landmarks, signs, logos, camera-facing smiles, and any implied medical outcome.

\#\#\# Recommended option

\*\*Option 1 — A moment at the height chart.\*\* A recognizable checkup activity immediately supports the copy about routine care and child development. The compact grouping works at phone width. The newborn scene is more specific to one age group, while the neighborhood scene relies on the headline to establish pediatric care.

Use generated imagery as generic editorial illustration and avoid suggesting it depicts the practice, an actual patient, or a particular office.
