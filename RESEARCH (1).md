### 2.4 Symptoms mentioned
**"Generally, kids with the following symptoms should be seen in the office":** [S5]
- Fever > 3 days (or any fever in an infant less than 6 months)
- Persistent vomiting or diarrhea
- Rashes
- Persistent pain such as stomach pain, headache, ear pain, or a sore throat
- Any unusual symptom that lasts more than 3 days

**Emergency — "call 911 and take your child to the nearest emergency room":** [S5]
- Difficulty breathing (not just noisy or congested)
- Extreme lethargy or difficulty staying awake
- Extreme irritability
- Dehydration
- Significant injury
- Severe pain not controlled by OTC pain medication
- Temperature >100.4 rectally in an infant less than 3 months old

### 2.5 Benefits mentioned (source-backed)
- **Prevention** via well-child visits: prevent "potential physical, mental, or social issues" (childhood diseases, sleep disorders, growth abnormalities, behavior disorders). [S4]
- **Continuity/relationship:** frequent early visits help parents "bond closely with their family pediatrician," increasing likelihood parents reach out with questions, "which can substantially improve the health of the child." [S4]
- **Access:** 365 days/year, medical professionals on call 24 hours/day. [S1, S2, S4, S5, S6]
- **Morning sick call without an appointment.** [S1, S2, S5]
- **One-stop convenience:** on-site rapid lab testing at each office; referrals to pediatric subspecialists, psychological services, and dietary evaluations "conveniently available at our location." [S2, S13, S16]
- **Parent support:** personalized education for parents and patients; patient educators (RNs trained in pediatric telephonic medicine) "a phone call away." [S2, S13]
- **Comfort:** "We take the time and care necessary to make your child as comfortable as possible" (physicals). [S6] Nurses calm children during exams. [S2]
- **Records continuity across offices:** EHR — "No matter which physician or location you use… your child's EHR is just a click away." [S16 — older brochure; current portal is athenahealth]
- **Digital convenience (established patients):** athenahealth patient portal and AthenaPatient™ app — messaging, appointments, immunization records, refills, bill pay, forms. [S14]
- **Self-scheduling:** Homepage banner: "You Now Have the Ability to Self Schedule a Well Child and Sick Illness Appointment in the Patient Portal!" [S1] (Details page could not be loaded.)

### 2.6 Service process / how it works

**A. Booking a routine appointment (well visit, physical)**
- Call (847) 295-1220 or request an appointment via the secure online form (/appointments/). [S4, S6]
- Established patients can self-schedule well-child and sick appointments in the patient portal. [S1, S14]

**B. Sick child**
1. Emergency? → call 911 / nearest ER (criteria in 2.4). [S5]
2. Unsure? → ask to speak with a patient educator, or use the online "Is Your Child Sick?" symptom checker. [S5]
3. Morning Sick Call: 8–9 am, no appointment needed — "register on the website from the parking lot." [S9, S10, S12]
   - Mon–Fri at all three offices; Saturday at Lindenhurst and Lake Bluff only. [S5]
4. After morning sick call → call 847-295-1220 for a sick appointment; "The more information you can provide us up front, the better we can serve your child." [S5]
5. During visit, pediatrician determines whether medication or lab testing is needed and discusses comfort during recovery. [S5]

**C. Expecting / new parents**
1. Get Acquainted Session — currently "recorded segments from our providers which cover some of the most frequently asked topics during prenatal visits." [S13] (Older brochure described in-person "get acquainted" tours and advised shopping for a pediatrician during the second trimester [S16] — **current site describes recorded segments; do not promise tours.**)
2. Tell the obstetrician and hospital staff you have chosen Lake Forest Pediatrics. [S13]
3. After hospital discharge, call 847-295-1220 to schedule baby's first visit. [S13]

**D. Well-child visit schedule (per AAP, as stated on S4)**
2–5 days; 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18 months; 2 years; 2½ years; then once yearly from age 3 until age 22. [S4]
⚠️ The Sports Physicals page lists a different infant/toddler schedule (2 weeks, 2, 4, 6, 9 months, 1 year; 18 months, 2 and 3 years). [S6] **Use S4 if a schedule is shown.**

**E. Physicals** [S6]
- School physical: year-over-year changes; younger years focus on development and vaccinations; adolescent years on psychological and physical changes; teens can ask about adolescent issues.
- Sports physical: vitals, joints, flexibility, vision; flags injury risks. Should be done **at least 6 weeks before the season.** Does **not** replace a school physical.
- Camp physical (pre-participation): may not always be required; does not replace a school physical.
- Before 1st grade, most schools require the child's first official physical; 4/5-year vaccines needed before kindergarten.
- School/daycare forms: can be sent via portal (PDF); signed forms retrievable in portal under My Health → Forms and Documents. [S14]
- Brochure: "School and sports physicals can be conveniently scheduled to meet school and team deadlines." [S16]
