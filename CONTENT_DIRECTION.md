# Content direction — General Pediatrics

## Primary message and visitor outcome

Help parents and caregivers find general pediatric care through Lake Forest Pediatrics and choose the office that works for them. The page should leave families with a clear sense of what the practice can help with, whom they are contacting, and what to do next.

Lead with general pediatric care and locality. Support that message with verified services, one specific care-team proof point, and practical answers about getting started. The strongest confirmed convenience is the ability to identify and contact the preferred office from one campaign page.

Write in a warm, direct voice for a caregiver comparing options on a phone. Use plain language and short explanations. Balance reassurance about the relationship with concrete details that help families make a decision. Use the headings and phrases below as drafting guidance.

## Search language and hero guidance

- **Approved search intent** — Finding a local pediatrician for ongoing care. The theme is inferred from the General Pediatrics objective and campaign discussion.
- **Candidate themes** — “Pediatrician near me,” “general pediatric care,” and “pediatrician in [office location].” Use natural service language and the selected office name. Candidate themes carry no implication about search volume or active targeting.
- **Supplied campaign scope** — General Pediatrics, with separate contact paths for Lake Bluff, Lindenhurst, and Vernon Hills. Keep the cold, flu, and RSV offer on the Seasonal and Illness Care page. Use other service themes only after the clinic confirms them and they support the campaign.
- **H1 approach** — Name general pediatric care in plain language, with a nearby office label such as “Lake Bluff office” when selected. Let the supporting line describe the practical benefit of finding the right care and contacting the right office. Verify the hero promise and CTA against the live ads before publication.

## Hierarchy and benefit framing

| Level | What belongs there | How to write it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline | General pediatric care and a visible office label | Let readers identify the service and location with a quick scan. |
| Section headlines | Services, the care team, how to get started, and how to contact the office | Make each heading answer a question a parent would bring to the page. |
| Short service entries | Well-child visits, immunizations, newborn care, and school or sports physicals | Connect well-child visits to discussing milestones, immunizations to staying on track with preventive care, newborn visits to raising early questions, and physicals to preparing for school or sports. |
| Supporting trust copy | One care-team feature and a short dedicated Google Reviews section | Draw from source-backed claims such as board-certified pediatricians, newborn through age 22 care, and more than 50 years serving local families. Use verified names, affiliations, and office assignments where relevant. Show confirmed Google rating and count with an approved profile link and preserve review wording. |
| Practical answers | New-patient availability, insurance, appointment process, and location details | Start with the answer and state which office or visit type it applies to when policies vary. Use a verified contact path when a caller must confirm details directly. |

Describe capabilities and likely practical benefits without guaranteeing appointment availability, medical outcomes, or continuity with a particular provider. Keep any unverified services, policies, affiliations, credentials, or patient quotes out of public copy until confirmed.

## Conversion copy

- **Primary action** — Use **Call [phone number]** with the selected office’s tracking number or **(847) 295-1220** while tracking numbers are pending. Use **Request Appointment** for the secondary popup action.
- **Before the form** — Put the heading **Request an Appointment** and the selected office in the GHL popup. Explain the verified next step. Describe appointment confirmation only when the workflow provides it. Avoid promising a response time until the practice verifies it.
- **Fields and feedback** — Use the practice’s approved fields and collect only what is needed to handle the inquiry. Keep medical details out of a general contact form unless the clinic supplies an approved intake process. Use visible labels, specific error messages, and a success message that accurately describes the status.
- **FAQ** — Prioritize whether the office is accepting new patients, how to request a visit, and how to verify insurance. Include only relevant, confirmed answers. Keep routine care questions on this page and send visitors with an immediate illness-related need to an approved contact path if the clinic wants that guidance.
- **Final CTA** — Restate the selected office, repeat the same action labels as the hero, and make switching offices available before submission.
